Whoever gets into your Whatomato account can send WhatsApp messages from your number. A password alone shouldn't be enough, so turn on two-step verification or add a passkey.

## Your account

An account is an email address and a password of 8 to 128 characters. Sign-up refuses a password that appears in a known data breach.

The account can't sign in until the address is verified through the emailed link, which works once, for 24 hours. Opened in the browser that signed up, the link also signs you in. Anywhere else it only verifies the address, so a link opened in someone else's browser can't sign them into your account. If the link has expired, the page it opens offers to send a new one.

A session lasts 30 days and extends while you use it.

A few things an account can't do yet: change its email address, change its password while signed in, list its signed-in devices, or delete itself. To change your password, use **Forgot your password?** on the sign-in page.

## Reset your password

On the sign-in page choose **Forgot your password?** and enter your email. The link in the email works once, for an hour.

<Screenshot name="forgot-password" alt="The Reset your password page, with a field for the account's email address and a Send reset link button" />

Saving a new password:

- signs out every device, including the one you're on;
- cancels every other emailed link and any sign-in that was waiting for a code;
- leaves two-step verification on and your passkeys working.

A reset is therefore also how you sign out a device you've lost. If someone else added a passkey to your account, remove it under **Settings** as well, since a reset doesn't.

## Two-step verification

With two-step verification on, signing in asks for your password and then a 6-digit code from an authenticator app on your phone. Any authenticator app works, such as 1Password, Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator or Authy.

### Turn it on

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings**. Under **Two-step verification**, choose **Turn on** and enter your password.

   <Screenshot name="settings-two-step" alt="The Two-step verification section of Settings, marked Off, with a Turn on button" />
2. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app. If you can't scan it, choose to enter a setup key in the app and type the key shown beside the code.

   <Screenshot name="two-step-setup" alt="The Set up your authenticator app dialog: a QR code, the setup key beside it, and a field for the code from the app" />
3. Enter the 6-digit code the app shows and choose **Turn on**.
4. Save the ten recovery codes somewhere safe. **Copy** and **Download** are there to help, and this is the only time the codes are shown. Tick **I saved these codes somewhere safe**, then choose **Done**.

</Steps>

A setup lasts 15 minutes and allows five wrong codes. Turning it on signs out every other device and sends you an email.

### Sign in with it

After your password, enter the code from the app. Each code works once. If you can't use the app, choose **Use a recovery code instead**; each recovery code also works once, and a sign-in with one emails you with how many are left.

The code step ends after five minutes or five wrong codes. Five wrong codes also email you, because whoever tried had your password. An account takes 30 wrong codes a day across all its sign-ins. Past that, every code is refused, even a right one, until the day is up or you reset your password.

### Recovery codes

Settings shows how many codes are left and warns you at three. **New recovery codes** replaces them all, used or not.

:::danger
If you lose both the authenticator app and your recovery codes, you can't get back in by yourself. Only whoever runs the instance can turn two-step verification off for you.
:::

### Turn it off

**Turn off** under **Two-step verification** asks for your password. Signing in then asks for your password only, and the recovery codes stop working.

## Passkeys

A passkey signs you in with your fingerprint, face or screen lock in place of your password. It only works on the site it was made for, so it can't be phished.

### Add one

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings**. Under **Passkeys**, choose **Add a passkey** and enter your password.

   <Screenshot name="settings-passkeys" alt="The Passkeys section of Settings with no passkeys yet and an Add a passkey button" />
2. Follow your device's prompt to create the passkey.
3. Give it a name that tells it apart from the others, such as "MacBook", or choose **Skip**.

</Steps>

An account holds up to 20 passkeys. A passkey can't be renamed afterwards; remove it and add it again.

### Sign in with one

On the sign-in page choose **Sign in with a passkey**, or pick the passkey when your browser offers it in the email field. There's no email or password to type.

<Screenshot name="signin" alt="The sign-in page, with a Sign in with a passkey button under the password form" />

A passkey always checks that it's you with a PIN or biometrics, so **a passkey sign-in asks for no two-step code**, even with two-step verification on.

### Remove one

**Remove** beside a passkey asks for your password. The passkey stops working at once.

Passkeys are tied to the address of the site. If an instance moves to another address, the passkeys made for the old one stop working.

## When your password is asked for again

Being signed in isn't enough for the things that matter most. These ask for your password again:

- linking and unlinking WhatsApp;
- turning two-step verification on or off, and creating new recovery codes;
- adding and removing a passkey;
- creating an [agent connection](/agents/connect/) and changing its permissions.

## Emails about your account

Whatomato emails you when two-step verification is turned on or off, when recovery codes are replaced or one is used, when a passkey is added or removed, when your password changes, and when five wrong codes follow a right password. If one arrives that you didn't cause, reset your password and review **Settings**.

## Sign-in limits

Sign-in attempts are limited so a password can't be guessed by trying: 10 attempts for an account, then one every 90 seconds, however many addresses they come from. If you see **Too many attempts**, wait for the time it gives. [Limits](/reference/limits/#accounts) lists the rest.
