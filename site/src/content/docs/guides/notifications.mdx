Notifications are about one thing: an [agent](/agents/overview/) asking for your approval. A notification names the agent and the chat, never the message, and a click on it opens the request. News about your WhatsApp link, such as an unlink or a ban, comes by email instead.

## Turn them on

Open **Settings**, find **Notifications** and choose **Turn on in this browser**. Your browser asks for permission. Once it's on, **Send a test** shows what a notification looks like.

<Screenshot name="settings-notifications" alt="The Notifications section of Settings, marked Off, with a Turn on in this browser button" />

Notifications are per browser. Turn them on in each browser you want to hear from; Settings lists them all, and you can turn any of them off from there. Signing out turns them off for that browser.

## On an iPhone or iPad

Notifications use the browser's own push service, and on iOS a website can only use it once it's on the Home Screen. In Safari, use **Share**, then **Add to Home Screen**, open Whatomato from there, and turn notifications on.

## Limits

- An account keeps its 10 most recent browsers. Turning notifications on in an eleventh drops the oldest.
- A browser notifies for one account: the one that turned notifications on in it last.
- A notification waits up to 24 hours for a browser that's offline.

## When it won't turn on

- **Notifications are blocked for this site**: allow them in your browser's settings for the site, then try again.
- **This browser can't show notifications from a website**: the browser doesn't support web push. On an iPhone or iPad, see above.
- **This browser's notification service isn't one Whatomato knows**: Whatomato sends only to the push services that Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge use.

Each notification is encrypted for the browser it goes to, so the push service that carries it can't read it.
